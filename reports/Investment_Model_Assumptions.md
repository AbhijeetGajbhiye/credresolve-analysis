# Investment Model Assumptions

The ₹10 Cr decision is intentionally a **planning hurdle**, not a causal forecast.

| Assumption | Treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Addressable volume | 67,104 annualized targeted account-month opportunities | Mean of the 7 complete Jan-Jul targeting months, annualized; August partial excluded |
| Primary statistical endpoint | 30-day payer rate | Payer rate is the endpoint used for the 80% power calculation and the pre-agreed lift hurdle |
| Financial decision metric | 30-day audited cash per eligible account | Converts experimental payer lift into financial value |
| Unit recovery per incremental payer | ₹77,866 | July audited SUCCESS cash divided by July audited payers **within the targeted July population** |
| Budget | ₹10 Cr | Assignment constraint |
| Hurdle | ~1.91 pp incremental payer lift | Budget divided by annualized opportunities x targeted-population recovery per payer |
| Holdout | 10% preferred operating point | Provides ~347 control accounts in the planning calculation; final randomization/power must be recalculated on actual eligible volume and baseline |
| Statistical planning assumptions | Two-sided alpha 5%, power 80% | Planning convention; final launch analysis should use observed baseline and variance |
| Causality | Historical pre/post is not causal | No historical randomized assignment exists |

### Interpretation

The model assumes an incremental payer generated by the intervention has recovery economics similar to the observed July targeted payer population. That is an explicit planning assumption, not an observed treatment effect.

The final scale gate is therefore:

> **Randomized incremental payer-rate lift >= ~1.91 pp, with acceptable 30-day cash/account economics and no guardrail breach.**

Downside, planning-case, break-even and upside scenarios are decision ranges rather than statistical confidence intervals.
